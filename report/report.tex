\Abstract{
This project develops a domain-specific chatbot for Slovenian investment and tax law. Since general-purpose LLMs lack reliable domain knowledge, the proposed approach uses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to produce accurate, up-to-date, and verifiable responses based on external legal documents. Prompt engineering is applied to enforce structure and introduce guardrails. The system is built on a curated dataset combining legal corpora and official Slovenian sources.
}

\section*{Introduction}
Large language models (LLMs) often lack sufficiently deep domain-specific understanding to provide reliable advice in specialized fields. In domain-specific applications responses must be both accurate and verifiable, enabling users to fact-check the information through clearly provided sources. Without such guarantees, the risks of misleading or unverifiable outputs limit the usability of LLM-based systems in high-stakes domains.

This project focuses on the development of a chatbot designed to provide guidance on Slovenian investment and tax law. The domain is highly specialized and subject to regulatory detail, making it challenging for general-purpose language models to deliver consistently accurate and up-to-date responses. As a result, additional mechanisms for injecting domain-specific knowledge are required.

This report presents the design, implementation, and evaluation of a pipeline for enhancing a general-purpose LLM with domain-specific knowledge. The goal is to enable the chatbot to produce accurate, grounded, and transparent responses suitable for advisory use.

\section*{Literature Overview}

Several prominent techniques exist for injecting domain-specific knowledge into large language models, differing in how knowledge is incorporated and maintained, as well as in their suitability for high-stakes domains such as law and finance.

A straightforward and widely adopted approach is prompt engineering~\cite{fewshot}, where model behavior is guided through carefully designed instructions, constraints, or examples. Rather than modifying the model itself, knowledge is implicitly introduced at inference time through the prompt. This makes the approach highly flexible and inexpensive, and particularly useful for rapid prototyping, enforcing output structure, or guiding reasoning processes in chatbot systems. However, its reliance on the context window limits how much domain knowledge can be injected, and performance can vary significantly depending on prompt formulation. As a result, prompt engineering alone is often insufficient in domains where correctness and reliability are critical.

In contrast, fine-tuning~\cite{lora} embeds domain knowledge directly into the model’s parameters by training on domain-specific data. This allows the model to internalize terminology, patterns, and expected behaviors, leading to more consistent and specialized outputs. Such properties are beneficial for tasks like document classification or maintaining a specific response style in a chatbot. At the same time, this approach comes with notable drawbacks: it is resource-intensive, depends on the availability of high-quality labeled data, and produces a static representation of knowledge that cannot easily adapt to updates, such as changes in regulations or financial markets. Furthermore, the lack of explicit source attribution limits its applicability in settings where transparency is required.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\cite{rag} addresses many of these limitations by combining pretrained model knowledge with external information sources. Instead of relying solely on internal parameters, the model retrieves relevant documents at inference time and conditions its responses on them. This enables access to up-to-date and domain-specific information, reduces hallucinations when retrieval is effective, and supports the inclusion of explicit references. Consequently, RAG is particularly well-suited for legal and financial chatbot systems, where answers must be both accurate and verifiable. The trade-off lies in increased system complexity, as it requires a dedicated retrieval pipeline, and in sensitivity to retrieval quality, which directly impacts response reliability and latency.

In practice, these methods are rarely used in isolation. Prompt engineering is often employed to structure and constrain outputs, fine-tuning to ensure consistent behavior, and RAG to provide grounded, up-to-date knowledge, resulting in hybrid systems that balance flexibility, accuracy, and transparency.





\section*{Proposed Methodology}
Chatbot systems designed for domains such as investing and tax law require a high level of accuracy, reliability, and transparency in their responses. To address these requirements, the proposed approach primarily relies on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). By integrating external domain-specific documents into the generation process, the system can provide up-to-date and factually grounded answers. Importantly, retrieved documents can be cited as references, allowing users to verify the information, which is essential in high-stakes decision-making contexts.

In addition to RAG, prompt engineering will be employed to guide the behavior of the language model. Carefully designed prompts will define the expected response structure, enforce clarity and precision, and introduce guardrails to reduce ambiguous or misleading outputs. This combination ensures that responses are not only grounded in reliable sources but also presented in a consistent and controlled manner.

\section*{Proposed Dataset and Data Sources}

In this project, we focus on developing a domain-specific AI assistant for the Slovenian legal domain, with a particular emphasis on tax and investment legislation (e.g., \textit{ZDoh-2}, \textit{ZDavP-2}). To achieve this, we construct a comprehensive dataset by combining an existing legal corpora with additional publicly available Slovenian legal and linguistic resources.

\subsection*{Core Legal Corpus}

The primary dataset is based on the Slovenian legal corpus provided within the LLM4DH project~\cite{llm4dh}. This corpus contains a large collection of Slovenian legislation, which serves as the main source of truth for the system. It includes structured legal documents such as laws, regulations, and amendments, making it suitable for tasks such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), legal question answering, and document grounding.

\subsection*{Supplementary Legal Data Sources}

To enhance the coverage and practical applicability of the system, we incorporate additional Slovenian legal resources:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PISRS (Pravno-informacijski sistem RS)}\footnote{\url{https://pisrs.si}}: A centralized repository of Slovenian legislation, providing structured access to laws, including tax-related acts such as ZDoh-2. This source enables article-level segmentation and metadata extraction.
    
    \item \textbf{Uradni list RS}\footnote{\url{https://www.uradni-list.si}}: The official gazette of the Republic of Slovenia, containing original publications of laws and amendments. This source is useful for validating legal texts and tracking legislative changes.
    
    \item \textbf{FURS (Financial Administration of RS)}\footnote{\url{https://www.fu.gov.si}}: Provides tax-related guidelines, explanations, and frequently asked questions. This resource is particularly relevant for constructing question-answer datasets aligned with real-world tax queries.
    
    \item \textbf{e-Uprava}\footnote{\url{https://e-uprava.gov.si}}: A government portal offering simplified explanations of legal procedures and regulations. This source is useful for bridging the gap between formal legal language and user-friendly descriptions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{CLARIN.SI Legal and Linguistic Corpora}

In addition to legal texts, we incorporate corpora from the CLARIN.SI infrastructure to support linguistic processing and model development.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Slovenian Legal Corpus (CLARIN.SI)}\footnote{\url{https://www.clarin.si/repository/xmlui/handle/11356/2095}}: This corpus contains Slovenian legal texts annotated with linguistic information and metadata. It is particularly useful for training and evaluating NLP models in the legal domain, as well as for tasks such as named entity recognition and terminology extraction.
    
    \item \textbf{siParl Parliamentary Corpus}\footnote{\url{https://www.clarin.si/repository/xmlui/handle/11356/1300}}: A large corpus of Slovenian parliamentary debates covering the period from 1990 to 2018, containing over 200 million words and rich metadata about speakers and sessions. This dataset provides contextual information about legislative discussions and policy development \cite{siparl}.
\end{itemize}

CLARIN resources are typically provided in structured formats such as XML or CONLL-U and include linguistic annotations (tokenization, lemmatization, part-of-speech tagging), which facilitates downstream NLP tasks and model fine-tuning.

\subsection*{Dataset Construction Strategy}

The final dataset will be constructed by combining the above sources into a unified corpus. The main steps include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data cleaning and normalization:} Converting documents from HTML/PDF/XML into a consistent textual format.
    \item \textbf{Segmentation:} Splitting legal documents into meaningful units (e.g., articles, paragraphs).
    \item \textbf{Metadata extraction:} Capturing information such as law title, article number, and publication date.
    \item \textbf{Domain filtering:} Selecting tax- and investment-related legislation (e.g., ZDoh-2).
    \item \textbf{Alignment with explanatory sources:} Linking legal provisions with interpretations from FURS and e-Uprava.
\end{itemize}

Overall, the combination of authoritative legal texts, explanatory government resources, and linguistically annotated corpora provides a strong foundation for building a reliable and domain-specific legal AI assistant.

\section{Submission 2 - Initial Solution and Results}

For the second submission we implemented an initial end-to-end baseline of the Slovenian tax and investment law chatbot. The system already covers the main components needed for retrieval-augmented generation: legal-document chunking, embedding, vector storage, neural reranking, prompt construction, local LLM serving, and an interactive command-line client. The implementation is located in the \texttt{chatbot} directory and is organized so that the indexing pipeline can be run separately from the online chatbot.

\subsection{System Architecture}

The baseline follows a modular RAG architecture. First, legal documents are processed offline into structured chunks and embedded into a retrieval store. At runtime, the chatbot embeds the user's question, retrieves candidate chunks from the store, reranks them with a cross-encoder, constructs a grounded prompt, and sends the final message sequence to a locally served instruction-tuned language model.

The chatbot is split into two main runtime processes. The script \texttt{run\_openai\_llm\_server.py} starts a \texttt{vLLM} server with an OpenAI-compatible \texttt{/v1/chat/completions} endpoint. It loads the model configured in \texttt{configs/config.yaml}; in the current configuration this is \texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}. The server writes its hostname, port, and model path into \texttt{configs/server\_boot\_config.yaml}. The client script \texttt{run\_chatbot\_client.py} then reads this file, starts \texttt{LawInvestmentChatbot}, loads the RAG components through \texttt{RAGHandler}, and provides an interactive terminal interface.

This separation is useful in the HPC environment because the LLM server can run as a non-interactive GPU job, while the chatbot client can run as a separate interactive job. The model and retrieval settings are centralized in \texttt{ChatbotSettings.py} and \texttt{configs/config.yaml}, which makes it possible to switch embedding models, rerankers, generation parameters, and RAG store paths without changing the main chatbot code.

For ease of development, most of the chatbot orchestration logic currently remains on the client side. The server is used mainly as a reusable OpenAI-compatible generation endpoint, while retrieval, reranking, prompt assembly, chat-history management, and response streaming are handled by the client. This choice makes iteration faster because the LLM server takes a relatively long time to load each time it is started; keeping the experimental logic in the client allows us to modify and test the RAG behavior without repeatedly restarting the model server.

\subsection{Chunking and Embedding Strategy}

The retrieval component of the chatbot is built by converting legal documents into structured text chunks and embedding them into a vector index. The full preprocessing pipeline is implemented in \texttt{run\_chunking\_and\_embedding.py}, while the legal-text parsing logic is implemented in \texttt{Chunking.py}. The input consists of COLESLAW-style JSONL records. Each record is converted into a \texttt{RawRecord} with fields such as \texttt{id}, \texttt{naziv}, \texttt{mopedId}, \texttt{eva}, \texttt{epa}, \texttt{sop}, and the full legal \texttt{text}.

The chunking process first filters the input records. In the default strategy, records are selected using a combination of explicitly whitelisted laws and keyword-based relevance filtering for tax and investment terminology. The whitelist currently includes core tax and finance laws such as \textit{ZDoh-2}, \textit{ZDavP-2}, \textit{ZDDPO-2}, \textit{ZDDV-1}, \textit{ZTFI-1}, \textit{ZISDU-3}, \textit{ZBan-3}, and \textit{ZGD-1}. The \texttt{--strict-whitelist} option keeps only these predefined laws, while the \texttt{--all-laws} option disables relevance filtering and processes every input record. Records that cannot be parsed into articles can optionally be preserved with \texttt{--keep-nonparsed}, in which case they are split into fallback document-level chunks.

Each selected legal document is normalized and segmented according to its internal legal structure. The parser removes common leading noise, detects article markers such as \textit{1. člen}, \textit{Člen 1}, and Roman-numeral article variants, extracts article headings when they appear in parentheses, and assigns chapter or section metadata from structural headings. If an article is longer than the configured limit, currently 1500 characters by default, the parser tries to split it along meaningful legal boundaries: internal article markers, numbered paragraphs such as \textit{(1)} and \textit{(2)}, numbered or lettered list items, and finally sentence-like punctuation or whitespace boundaries. These paragraph and list markers are currently used mainly as split boundaries; the normal article chunks do not yet preserve paragraph numbers as separate metadata fields.

Each generated chunk contains the raw chunk text and structured metadata. The metadata includes the chunk identifier, record identifier, law title, law code, document identifiers, chapter, article number, article label, optional article heading, optional chunk part for split articles, source type, and domain tags such as \texttt{tax}, \texttt{income\_tax}, \texttt{capital\_gains}, \texttt{investment}, \texttt{corporate\_tax}, or \texttt{vat}. A formatted version of the chunk is also created by combining the legal text with its metadata. This formatted representation is used for embedding so that retrieval can benefit from both the content of the provision and its legal context.

Embeddings are generated with a configurable sentence-transformer model. The preprocessing script defaults to \texttt{intfloat/multilingual-e5-base} and prefixes embedded chunks with \texttt{passage:}, which is appropriate for E5-style retrieval models. In the current chatbot configuration, the embedding model is set to \texttt{BAAI/bge-m3}; this can be passed to the preprocessing script through the \texttt{--model} argument when rebuilding the RAG store. Embeddings are normalized by default, stored as a float32 NumPy matrix in \texttt{embeddings.npy}, and can optionally be skipped with \texttt{--chunks-only}. Unless disabled with \texttt{--no-faiss}, the script also writes \texttt{index.faiss}. The default FAISS metric is cosine similarity, implemented as inner product search over normalized vectors, with L2 search available as an alternative.

The output directory contains \texttt{chunks.jsonl}, \texttt{embeddings.npy}, \texttt{index.faiss}, and \texttt{report.json}. The report records the input path, output paths, embedding shape, number of processed, kept, skipped, faulty, and chunked records, filtering reasons, top laws by records and chunks, domain tag counts, and the preprocessing configuration. This strategy balances retrieval precision with legal interpretability: chunks are constrained by legal structure where possible, while the attached metadata preserves the source context needed for citation and verification.

\subsection{Retrieval, Reranking, and Prompt Construction}

At inference time, \texttt{RAGHandler.py} loads \texttt{chunks.jsonl} and \texttt{embeddings.npy} from the configured RAG store. The user's question is embedded with the same sentence-transformer family used for the indexed chunks. Candidate retrieval is currently implemented by matrix multiplication between the normalized embedding matrix and the query vector. The system retrieves the top 50 candidate chunks according to embedding similarity.

The initial candidates are then reranked with a cross-encoder. In the current configuration the reranker is \texttt{BAAI/bge-reranker-large}. For each candidate, the reranker receives the user question and a short chunk representation consisting of the law title and chunk text. The reranked list is sorted by cross-encoder score, and the top 10 chunks are inserted into the final RAG context.

The prompt construction step is intentionally strict. Each retrieved block contains an index, law title, article label, optional paragraph number, optional chunk part, and the legal text. The generated RAG instruction tells the model to answer only from the provided context, cite the relevant law and article, mention exceptions only if they are visible in the retrieved text, and explicitly state when the answer cannot be reliably determined from the context. This is combined with the persistent Slovenian system prompt in \texttt{configs/system\_prompt.yaml}, which enforces a formal legal-advisory style and a fixed answer structure: short summary, detailed explanation, relevant legal sources, and limitations or warnings.
